# c2 refuter review: South West France and Corsica

Two articles were checked in `france.more.json` against standard knowledge, the Codex's own Village, Premier Cru and Grand Cru banks and primers, the Court's south-france rows and four searches (two for each article). Both are sound and readable at the Village level, in the house style of the c1 neighbour. Twelve fixes were made in place, and nothing that was right was rewritten.

## Fixes by kind
- **Depth tags (5):** Saussignac and Pacherenc du Vic-Bilh are asked in the Codex's Village bank, so the two places lose their tag, as do Pacherenc's card and question. Coteaux du Cap Corse goes from master to advanced, beside the other four named zones that the advanced card and question ask together. Two untagged Corsica exam lines prescribed tagged facts at Village, Sciaccarello as Mammolo and the five named zones; each now says the fact is asked from Premier Cru.
- **Question form (4):** in three questions the key was the longest option. A distractor of the same kind replaced the shorter one: South West Q4 (Noble rot from autumn mists), South West Q6 (Gave de Pau, the explanation teaching it) and Corsica Q2 (Cabernet Sauvignon, grown for IGP Île de Beauté). In Corsica Q5 the bare Muscat left the accept list, since the stem's wine name gives it away.
- **Facts and precision (3):** Patrimonio's limestone now sits on an island of granite and schist, not a granite island (trap, card and place note). The South West's IGPs are no longer counted as two. Madiran's rules are homologated anew on 26 November 2025, not last homologated, so the line will not go stale.

## Confirmed by search
- Madiran's amended text, adopted on 12 June 2025: Tannat the main grape, with Cabernet Franc, Cabernet Sauvignon, Fer and Manseng Noir accessory.
- The 1936 dates: Monbazillac among the first six AOCs (15 May 1936), Jurançon by the decree of 8 December 1936, and Bergerac among the appellations turning ninety in 2026.
- Ajaccio's 40 per cent Sciaccarello in the 2011 and 2019 texts, and Rappu as an Aleatico sweet red of Cap Corse outside the four AOCs.

## The Court's rows
Every south-france row the Court writes for Southwest France and the Dordogne is covered: the location, the continental category, the Pyrenees and landlocked, both Mansengs, the four reds, Cahors, Madiran, Jurançon (Blanc Sec and the Court's off-dry Blanc), Bergerac, Monbazillac, the 125 BC line and both pairings. No CMS subject writes a row for Corsica.

## Left open
- Fronton's Négrette and Sciaccarello as Mammolo are asked only in the Grand Cru bank; both stay advanced.
- Monbazillac as one of the first six AOCs would make a good exam line for a later edition.
- Côt's respelling, KOHT, stands; French usage varies.
