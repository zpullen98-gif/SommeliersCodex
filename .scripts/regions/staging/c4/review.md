# c4 refuter review: Liguria and Valle d'Aosta, Calabria and Basilicata

Two articles were checked in `italy.more.json` against standard knowledge, the Codex's own Régionale, Village, Premier Cru and Grand Cru banks, the Piedmont and Campania fragments beside them, the Court's central-southern-italy and tuscany rows and seven searches (three for Liguria and Valle d'Aosta, four for Calabria and Basilicata). Both are sound and readable at the Village level. Twelve fixes were made in place, and nothing that was right was rewritten.

## Fixes by kind
- **Depth tags (3):** Donnas and Carema's stone-pillared pergolas lose their master tag on the card and the question, since the Village bank asks Carema's pergolas at the Valle d'Aosta border and ties Donnas to it. The card of the Superiore's ageing figures is tagged advanced, as the article's own exam line and question already placed them. An untagged card no longer teaches the count of seven sub-zones that the tagged card and question ask.
- **Exam lines and cards brought into line (2):** Liguria's Grand Cru exam line now names its real master places, Golfo del Tigullio-Portofino and Colline di Levanto, and the Carema link joins the Village grapes line. In Calabria the Riserva's five years leaves the untagged card and exam line, and the Premier Cru line names the Superiore's and the Riserva's ageing years.
- **Facts and precision (4):** the Valle d'Aosta lies beyond Piedmont, not over Liguria's mountains. Etna and Vesuvius are no longer the only active volcanoes of southern Italian wine. Grottino di Roccanova's Malvasia white is named. The IGT tier now says IGP is the European Union's term, the Court's word.
- **Respellings (2):** Dora Baltea is DOH-rah BAHL-teh-ah, and Matera is MAH-teh-rah.
- **Sources (1):** the Rossese di Dolceacqua disciplinare (DPR 28 January 1972) is added as a primary source.

## Confirmed by search
- Cirò Classico DOCG, 2025, Calabria's first: dry red only, at least 90 per cent Gaglioppo with Magliocco or Greco Nero, from Cirò and Cirò Marina, 36 months with six in wood.
- Aglianico del Vulture Superiore: DOCG of 2010, three years with twelve months in wood and twelve in bottle, Riserva from the fifth year, 13.5 per cent; the DOC of 1971.
- Cirò DOC of 2 April 1969; Rossese di Dolceacqua of 28 January 1972, Liguria's first DOC; Ormeasco di Pornassio a DOC of its own from 2003.
- Blanc de Morgex et de La Salle: Prié Blanc only, ungrafted, on low pergolas up to about 1,200 metres.

## The Court's rows
Every central-southern-italy row that touches these places is covered: Basilicata and Calabria placed between their seas, the Mediterranean south against the cooler Vulture, the Apennines, the Ligurian and Tyrrhenian seas, Aglianico, IGP, DOC and DOCG, Superiore and Riserva, producers and the decanting line. The tuscany row's Ligurian Sea sits in Liguria's water. No CMS subject writes a row for Liguria's wines or the Valle d'Aosta.

## Left open
- The Valle d'Aosta as Italy's smallest wine region stays: it is measured by area and by output, and the Régionale bank asks it.
- Cirò Classico's 13 per cent and its yield are left out, and the Valle d'Aosta DOC stays undated.
- Sciac-trà stays a rosé, as the reference works give it, against one search summary that called it red.
- Fumin and Donnas keep their French respellings; Italian usage also stresses the first syllable.
- The aliases barile, monte vulture and rionero in vulture still point to campania; the integrator may move them.

## The gate
`check-fragment` fails only on the one rule this edition sets aside: an article off the map needs a deeper list. A copy with only that rule relaxed passes the file whole, with no superlative note. The integrator relaxes the rule.
