# The Court's first two examinations, as published in October 2026

What the Court of Master Sommeliers, Americas, says today about its Introductory Sommelier Course and Examination and its Certified Sommelier Examination, set against what the Codex says about the levels it calls Régionale and Village. Researched on 11 October 2026.

## How this was established, and how far to trust it

* Twelve web searches, ten of them limited to mastersommeliers.org, were run on 11 October 2026. Reading the Court's pages directly failed in this session (the host would not resolve), so every fact below rests on search extracts of the Court's own pages and PDFs as the search engine indexed them. Exact wording, and any "last updated" line on a page, could not be seen.
* The Court's PDFs sit in dated upload folders. Most of the candidate guides are in the December 2023 folder (wp-content/uploads/2023/12), the online Introductory exam guide in April 2024 and the Advanced and Master service standards in May 2025. A file in an old folder can be replaced in place, so a folder date says when the file was first uploaded, not that it is out of date.
* Where the Court's own pages disagree, the live certification page is preferred to an older PDF, and the disagreement is stated.
* Everything here is in this document's own words. Nothing is copied from a published question bank or a book.

## The Introductory Sommelier Course and Examination

**Source:** the Court's Introductory course page (mastersommeliers.org/certification/certification-levels/introductory-sommelier-course-and-exam/), the Introductory Course Guide and Introductory Course Syllabus (uploads 2023/12), the curriculum guide shared with the Certified theory (uploads 2023/12), the online course pages and the Online Introductory Sommelier Examination Guide (uploads 2024/04), and a 2026 online course listing (mastersommeliers.org/courses/introductory-sommelier-course-and-examination-4-3/).

**The course.** It is offered two ways.

* **In person:** two full days of class (roughly eight in the morning to six in the evening each day), with the examination at about four in the afternoon on the second day, followed by a reception where results are given.
* **Online:** self paced.

The course is a fast review of the world's wines and other drinks, with several tastings and demonstrations of wine service along the way. Candidates are expected to have studied the course workbook before they arrive. No earlier course or trade experience is required; candidates must be 21 or over.

**The examination.**

* 70 multiple choice questions in 45 minutes, on the content of the course and its workbook.
* The pass mark is 60 per cent, which is 42 correct answers.
* The online route sets the same size of paper and the same time. It is invigilated live by webcam on a desktop or laptop computer; the 2024 guide names the invigilation service and a locked browser. The online score is shown at once.

**What it examines, and what it does not.**

* **Examined:** wine law, which grapes belong to which regions, how wines are made, figures from wine history, food and wine pairing, finding places on a map, reading labels, basic service knowledge and basic spirits.
* **Not examined:** tasting, carrying out service, or memorising vintages.

So the Introductory has one examined part, the theory paper.

**What passing gives.** A certificate, a lapel pin and the right to sit the Certified Sommelier Examination.

**The curriculum topics** (the Court's curriculum guide for the Introductory and the Certified theory):

* **Viticulture:** location and geography, climate, topography, soil, vineyard management and harvest decisions.
* **Vinification:** the decisions made from harvest to bottling, basic wine chemistry, barrels, fining and filtration, and packaging.
* **The wine regions of the world:** for each, the law, producers, vintages, history and food pairing.
* **Beer, sake and spirits** at a basic level.
* **The sommelier's skills:** the deductive tasting method, food and drink pairing, the business of the sommelier, and hospitality and service.

**Not confirmed.** How long online access lasts: one Court page says 180 days, and a 2026 listing offers 90 days. One 2026 listing describes the online course as 29 lessons and 70 topics, but that figure was seen only in a search extract. Fees are left out on purpose, since they change.

## The Certified Sommelier Examination

**Source:** the Court's Certified page (mastersommeliers.org/certification/certification-levels/certified-sommelier-examination/), the Certified Sommelier Exam Guide and the Certified Sommelier Exam Syllabus (uploads 2023/12), the Certified deductive tasting grid (uploads 2023/12), the exam FAQ, the Hospitality and Service Standards (uploads 2023/12), the 2025 Advanced and Master Hospitality and Service standards (uploads 2025/05), and the Court's candidate portal page for the Certified examination.

**Shape.** One day with three separately scored sections:

1. a deductive tasting;
2. a theory paper, which includes the business of the sommelier;
3. a hospitality and service practical.

**Who may sit.** Only candidates who have passed the Introductory. The Court recommends at least three years in the drinks and hospitality trade.

**Equipment.** The theory and the tasting are both taken on Examplify. Candidates bring their own Windows or Mac laptop or an iPad; Chromebooks, Android devices, iPhones and Linux are not supported.

**The theory.**

* 45 questions in 38 minutes.
* The formats are multiple choice, short answer, matching and simple arithmetic.
* The business questions expect basic costing for a wine programme, such as the cost of goods sold and the cost percentage.

**The tasting.**

* **The wines:** four, two white and two red.
* **The task:** describe and identify each by the Court's deductive tasting method.
* **The form:** written, not spoken. Candidates note their observations on paper and enter them into Examplify.
* **The time:** 45 minutes. The paper submits itself when the time is up, and no extra time is given to finish typing. The guide suggests about eleven minutes a wine, with the whites finished by halfway.

The published Certified grid for a white asks for:

* the structure: sweetness, acidity, alcohol and the rest;
* the grape;
* the climate;
* the country;
* an age range;
* a short sale of the wine to a guest, in two sentences or fewer.

The sample day schedule in the December 2023 syllabus allows only 30 minutes for the tasting. That looks older than the 45 minute rule now stated on the Court's page, in the guide and on the candidate portal.

**The service practical.**

* **The setting:** close to a real restaurant. The candidate shows salesmanship and knowledge while carrying out tableside tasks.
* **What may be asked:** opening still or sparkling wine, recommending cocktails or spirits, and discussing which wines suit which dishes.
* **The syllabus's skill list:** sparkling and still wine served by the bottle and by the glass, selling a pairing, and knowledge of cocktails, beer, aperitifs, digestifs and fortified wines.
* **What to bring:** candidates arrive dressed and equipped for restaurant wine service, with their own corkscrew, a pen and paper.

**The service standards.** The Court says its Hospitality and Service Standards are the only service standards it accepts at any of its examinations.

* **The 2023 standards cover:**
  * presenting and opening sparkling wine (three methods, with a fourth at a guéridon);
  * decanting for sediment, with a candle or light, a cradle, and the pour stopped when the sediment reaches the shoulder;
  * service temperatures, with sparkling at 42 to 50°F (5.5 to 10°C).
* **The 2025 Advanced and Master standards add:**
  * the three reasons to decant: air, sediment and temperature;
  * splash and double decanting;
  * a second serviette for sparkling wine, used to wipe the lip.

**Pass marks.**

* At least 60 per cent in each of the three sections.
* All three must be passed at the same sitting.
* Results come as pass or no pass for each section, followed by a feedback report within ten business days.

## What could not be confirmed

* **When the tasting changed.** The Court now states four wines in 45 minutes, but no announcement or date of the change was found. That the Certified tasting was once two wines, one white and one red, in about 25 minutes rests on knowledge from before this research, not on a source found here. The 30 minute slot in the 2023 syllabus suggests an intermediate or older form.
* **Decanting at the Certified.** The Certified pages found name opening still or sparkling wine, not decanting. Decanting is in the Court's standards, and the Court's Advanced guide tells candidates to drill both sparkling service and decanting. Whether a Certified candidate may be asked to decant could not be settled.
* **The detail of the service section.** The number of tasks, the minutes at the table and how marks are shared out are not published in anything found.
* **The sale on the grid.** The two sentence sale was seen in the published grid only through a search extract. Whether it is scored in the current tasting is not confirmed.
* **Resitting one section.** The current page says all sections must be passed within one sitting. No rule was found that lets a candidate keep a passed section, and none is assumed here.
* **The online Introductory.** The access period and the number of lessons, as above.
* **Advanced and Master.** These were outside this research, and nothing here confirms or corrects the Codex's figures for them.

## How the Codex compares

The findings, each with its file, line, current words, proposed words and source, are in `.scripts/review/findings/exam.json`. In short:

* **Régionale (the Introductory).**
  * The level note's 70 questions in 45 minutes, all multiple choice, with 60 per cent to pass, is right.
  * The home blurb is right.
  * Treating it as theory alone (`EXAM_SECTIONS.intro`) is right.
* **Village (the Certified): the theory.**
  * 45 questions in 38 minutes at 60 per cent is right.
  * The level note and the home blurb leave out the simple arithmetic questions.
  * The blurb describes only the theory paper, with nothing of the tasting or the service.
* **Village: the readiness model.**
  * `EXAM_SECTIONS.certified` (theory, tasting, service) is right.
  * The 60 per cent bars for theory and tasting are right.
  * The readiness words still carry long dashes; the site's copy of the same file already has them out.
* **The Village tasting (codex39).** This is the largest discrepancy. The Codex deals two wines in 25 minutes, but the Court sets four wines, two white and two red, in 45 minutes, written and submitted automatically at the end. The Finals' grid in codex21 deals four wines but does not hold them to two of each colour.
* **The service rites (codex6).**
  * The rites match the Court's description in substance.
  * The questions asked mid-service leave out beer, aperitifs, digestifs and fortified wines, which the syllabus names.
  * The purpose of decanting leaves out temperature, which the 2025 standards name.
  * The link text for the 2023 document could use its own title.
* **The Finals (codex19).** Forty questions with no clock is a deliberate design, a broad check rather than a copy of the Court's paper, so it is not a discrepancy. If the Village test were ever meant to mirror the day itself, the paper would be 45 questions and the grid two whites and two reds.
